# Vanvani

### A Digital Initiative for Tribal Language Preservation

Vanvani is a conceptual digital platform designed to explore how technology can be used to help preserve and promote tribal and indigenous languages and the cultural knowledge associated with them.

The idea came from looking at language not simply as a means of communication, but as an important part of a community's history, identity, traditions, and collective knowledge. Many tribal languages face challenges such as declining numbers of speakers, limited documentation, and reduced transmission between generations. Vanvani explores one possible digital approach to addressing this problem.

---

## The Idea

Vanvani is envisioned as a platform where linguistic and cultural resources can be documented, explored, and shared.

The proposed platform could include resources such as:

* **Words and phrases** in tribal languages
* **Audio recordings** of native speakers
* **Stories and oral traditions**
* **Translations and meanings**
* **Traditional knowledge and cultural information**
* **Community-contributed content**
* **Learning resources** for younger generations

An important part of the concept is the involvement of the communities themselves. Rather than treating tribal languages as information that is simply collected and stored, Vanvani is envisioned as a platform where speakers and community members can contribute to and engage with their own linguistic and cultural heritage.

The broader aim is to support both **documentation and continued use of these languages**, particularly across generations.

---

## The Prototype

This repository contains the initial **HTML-based prototype** of Vanvani.

The prototype was created to visualise how the proposed platform could look and function. It demonstrates the basic interface, navigation, organisation of information, and possible user experience of the application.

It is a **conceptual prototype**, not a fully functional application. There is currently no backend, database, authentication system, or real community-contribution system connected to it.

The purpose of the prototype is to communicate the idea and demonstrate how it could potentially be developed into a functional digital platform.

## Academic Background

Vanvani was originally developed as an academic project and presented at **NATIONAL SEMINAR in collaboration with ICSSR, NEW DELHI on Community-Initiated Digital and Cultural Innovations for Preserving and Promoting Santali, Mundari, and Ho Languages in Eastern India: Implications for Multilingual Education and NEP 2020** at Vasanta College for Women in April 2026.

The project grew from an interest in the relationship between **geography, tribal communities, language, culture, and technology**.

The idea was subsequently developed further into a written contribution on tribal language preservation.

---

## Why Vanvani?

Digital technology can make information easier to store, access, and share, but language preservation is not simply a matter of putting words into a database.

A language is connected to the people who speak it, the places where it is used, and the stories, traditions, and knowledge carried through it.

Vanvani therefore explores the possibility of combining **digital documentation with community participation**, with the intention of making tribal languages more visible and accessible while keeping their cultural context in mind.

## Future Development

This prototype represents an initial exploration of the Vanvani concept.

A future full-scale implementation could potentially include a proper database, audio recording and playback, community contributions, searchable language resources, learning tools, translation features, and other functionality for documenting and engaging with tribal languages.

Such an implementation would require further research and technical development, as well as careful consideration of community participation, consent, cultural sensitivity, and the ethical handling of linguistic and cultural resources.
